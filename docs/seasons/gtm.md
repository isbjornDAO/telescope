# GTM

**For:** shipped products built to scale and retain users. The traditional hackathon, done properly.

GTM has a clear winner. At the end of the season the community chooses its **Victor**: the crew whose product is named the best shipped tool of the season. Default settlement is Avalanche (C-Chain, an L1, or Iggy L1); Chainlink, Midnight, and other destinations are allowed — Avalanche is the primary engine, not a tourist filter.

## Format

Crews submit a working product with a live link and a destination (`deployedOn`). Submissions are public. The season runs as a bracket: knockout rounds decided by community vote against a threshold, then finals.

## What makes it better than a normal hackathon

- **Watchable.** Live brackets, faction supporters, scouts commenting on matchups, regions rallying behind their crews. The season is a spectator sport.
- **Insightful.** Every entry publishes its roadmap and its metrics as it goes. Judging is not a demo day; it is six weeks of visible building.
- **Interactive.** The community does not just vote at the end. It uses the products, files feedback, and its usage counts.
- **Honest about retention.** The Victor's standing only fully vests at 90 days if the product still has its users.
- **Conservation through-line.** Every season maps to an Arctic conservation journey — the reason missions exist, not a charity banner.

## Who decides

The community, weighted by trust. See [Voting and knockouts](voting-and-knockouts.md). Votes from nodes with no path to an Anchor carry no weight.

## The Victor

The Victor gets the season title, the largest standing allocation of the season, a treasury allocation, and the Victor's seat: the product Telescope and Team1 put forward for that season, with the marketing and introductions that come with it. **(proposal)** When the product settles on Avalanche, the Victor is also Telescope's nominated project for Avalanche Foundation programmes that season.
