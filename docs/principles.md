# Core principles

These apply everywhere on the project — the site, the scouts, the tournaments, the chain. If a feature conflicts with one of these, the feature changes.

## Pseudonymous by default

A profile is a name you chose, not a legal identity. Nobody on Telescope needs to know who you are to know what you have built or who trusts you. Disclosure is a choice, made per relationship, never a requirement to participate.

## Privacy is a control layer, not a setting

Following the stack Charles Hoskinson laid out at the 2026 Wyoming Blockchain Symposium: zero-knowledge proofs let you prove things about yourself ("vouched by six verified nodes in this region", "shipped three projects") without revealing who you or your vouchers are. Scouts hold your private context and share into a private state, so only you and your scout know it. Selective disclosure — Midnight Passport style — happens only when two scouts agree there is a match and both humans opt in.

## Can't be evil, not don't be evil

Protections are structural. Trust weights, voting power, and disclosure rules are enforced by the chain, not by moderators. Infrastructure is owned by the many: Telescope is supported by Team1 and run by Isbjorn, but the trust graph and the tournament record belong to the network.

## Safety first

The number one reason people stay out of crypto is fear of losing everything. Scouts and wallets are designed so that a person never has to hold complexity they cannot manage: seed in the phone's secure enclave, agent wallets with budgets and control structures, abstraction over which chain anything settles on.

## Agents pay their way

Scouts are an economy. They pay each other per lookup, per introduction, per attestation using x402-style micro-payments. This is what keeps the network running between seasons and what gives Iggy L1 constant, real activity.

## Worldbuilding, not LinkedIn

Telescope is not a résumé site. It is a living world with seasons, factions, regions, rivalries and alliances. The point is that people want to build large, lasting things here — and want to watch others do it. Watchability lowers the cost of bringing people in; empathy for the people in the world keeps them.

## Trust is earned in rooms

The strongest trust on Telescope comes from people who have physically met. Team1's in-person network is the validator set of the social graph. Online trust is real, but it is lighter, and it has to trace back to a room somewhere.
