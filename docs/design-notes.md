# Design notes

Taken from the r/Design thread "What's the most beautifully designed website
you've seen?" Only what commenters actually praised, and what it means for
Telescope. Kept short on purpose.

## What the thread rewarded

**McMaster-Carr, the runaway favourite.** Called "the most purely minimal and
deeply functional website ever created." Two things people singled out:

- **It is fast.** Fast enough that a separate thread exists picking apart how.
  Speed was discussed as a design property, not an engineering one.
- **You never need the search box.** The navigation is good enough that you find
  the thing by browsing. And when you do search, backing out to Google does not
  give a better answer. Almost no site can say that.

**vacation.inc.** Praised for "the word *Bag* instead of an icon", typography and
colour sense, and being "actual originality without being a bunch of slop thrown
on a page."

**motherfuckingwebsite.com.** Radical plainness, admired. Also immediately
critiqued: *"Line height on those paragraphs isn't too pleasant."* Plain is not an
excuse for unreadable.

**Cameron's World, Space Jam 1996, GeoCities.** Loved for personality, not
polish. "When the internet was a living, organic place." A site is allowed to
have a character.

**FerrumPipe.** One long scroll-driven sequence. What people praised was not the
effect but the care: "the execution is so smooth and flawless. Someone really
cares about the work they do."

**The general rule someone stated outright:** most sites called beautiful are
beautiful because of their images or video, and *"the designer just needs to stay
out of the way of the visual elements."*

**What got attacked:** "AI slop", and sites that are really just a company
quietly steering you toward its own product.

## What this means here

1. **Content on arrival.** McMaster drops you into the catalogue. Telescope
   should drop you into the conversation. Anything above the first post is rent
   the reader pays before getting anything.
2. **Words beat icons.** Label the tabs. An icon is a puzzle; a word is an
   answer.
3. **Navigation good enough to not need search.** Every board visible, ordered
   by what is alive right now.
4. **Speed is part of the look.** A page that appears instantly feels better made
   than one that fades in prettily.
5. **Readability is not optional.** Line height, measure, and contrast get
   deliberate values, not defaults.
6. **Keep the bear.** Personality is what people remembered about the sites they
   loved. Restraint applies to decoration, not to character.
7. **Get out of the way.** The posts are the content. Frames, shadows and blur
   compete with them.

## The test

Open the site on a phone. If you cannot read a real post without scrolling, the
design is wrong, however nice it looks.
